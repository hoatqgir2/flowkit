## ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### CONTENT & SCRIPT DNA
- **Niche:** Ordinary daily life in civilizations YouTube barely covers — not Rome, not Egypt, not Viking raids, not "ancient humans vs giant animals." The viewer is dropped into one normal day of a named people: eating, washing, trading, sleeping, raising a child, working a boat, a field, a market, or a kiln.
- **Home civilizations:** Srivijaya and the Orang Laut, Khmer village life around Angkor, Mali and the salt roads, Songhai river towns, Heian Japan from a commoner's or minor court attendant's day, Aztec daily life that is not sacrifice, the Kingdom of Aksum, Champa, Đại Việt village life when the evidence is solid, Swahili coast trading towns, the Nabataeans at Petra beyond the facade, the Mississippian town of Cahokia. One people per video.
- **Format:** 7–12 minute educational explainer narrated in calm, intelligent 2nd-person ("you", "your boat", "your child", "your market") — never "we" or "I".
- **Hook formula:** Open by dropping the viewer inside a sensory morning ("You wake to woodsmoke and river mud. No bell. No clock.") → name the place and the year in the next two lines → contrast it against the version of that civilization the viewer thinks they know (temples, kings, treasure) and pivot to the ordinary person.
- **Script rhythm:** Short sentence. Short sentence. One longer sentence that builds depth. Short sentence. Question?
- **Narrative arc:** Hook → Name the person, place, and century → Evidence stack (a named chronicle, inscription, archaeologist, or site) → Reconstruct one concrete day the viewer can picture → Counterintuitive twist (the "lost" civilization was a normal working town; the famous ruin was not the whole life) → Modern mirror → Closing line that echoes the first line, completely reframed.
- **Evidence rule:** Weave at least 3 real named sources naturally into the narration: an archaeologist, a chronicle, an inscription, or a site. Safe anchors include Zhou Daguan on Angkor, Ibn Battuta where he actually went, the Kedukan Bukit inscription for Srivijaya, George Coedès only when the claim is the old identification and not a new fact, al-Umari and Ibn Khaldun on Mali with care, the Periplus of the Erythraean Sea for Aksum trade, Peter Bellwood only for Austronesian context. Never invent a name, a quote, a date, or a custom. If historians disagree, say so in one plain sentence.
- **No jargon without plain-English explanation.** Decode mandala, serf, corvée, stilt house, monsoon, cowrie, laterite the moment each word appears.
- **Always ends** by reflecting that ordinary day back onto something the viewer does today: a commute, a meal, a bath, a debt, a child waking them.
- **Do not exoticize.** These people are not mystical, not primitive, and not a backdrop for a king's treasure. The story is the workday.

### PROVEN VIRAL TOPIC ANGLES
1. "What a Day Was Like in ___" — one ordinary person, one place, one century.
2. "How Did Ordinary People in ___ Actually Live?" — food, sleep, work, children. Not the king.
3. "Why You Wouldn't Last a Week as a ___" — a real job: salt trader, river pilot, temple cook, kiln worker, monsoon sailor.
4. "The City You've Never Heard Of" — a capital or port that does not have a doodle channel yet.
5. "What ___ Did All Day" — a role the thumbnail can show in one pose: boat, salt bar, market scale, rice field.
6. "The Version of ___ Nobody Shows You" — temple or gold on the left with a red X, the workday on the right.

### CIVILIZATION RULES
- One people, one century, one job per video. Do not tour five empires.
- Avoid the saturated defaults: Rome, Egypt, Greece, Vikings, generic medieval peasants, generic Stone Age.
- Prefer a port, a river town, a market, or a village over a throne room.
- If a famous ruin appears, it is the background of someone's workday, not the subject.

### VISUAL STYLE DNA
- **Art style:** Hand-drawn 2D doodle cartoon animation — flat solid colors, bold black hand-drawn outlines, slightly wobbly/imperfect lines as if sketched fast with a marker. Childlike and simple. Minimal interior line detail. ZERO gradients, ZERO drop shadows, ZERO photographic textures.
- **Hero (locked per video):** The same stick figure in every frame of that video. Large round white head, two small black dot eyes, thin black eyebrows, a simple black line mouth, thin black stick limbs, small black mitten hands, no nose, no ears. Hair is the civilization lock and must not change: messy dark-brown hair for a river or field worker, hair in a simple knot for a market woman, a flat single-color head cloth when the culture used one. One flat clothing color for the whole video (ochre, indigo, or red). Bare feet unless the job needs sandals.
- **Modern mirror:** Only in the closing beat. Same stick-figure body, but spiky bright ORANGE hair (#F58220), five short spikes, no historical clothes. Never mix the orange hair into the historical scenes.
- **Objects:** Chunky and flat. A boat, a salt bar, a rice bundle, a market scale, a stilt house, a clay jar, a laterite block. Thick black outlines. One prop is the signature of the video and must look the same every time it appears.
- **Backgrounds (flat solid color zones):**
- Cream = concept text frames.
- Light blue sky + tan ground + green trees = inland day.
- Blue water band + tan bank + one stilt house = river or port.
- Orange sky + tan ground = dawn, dusk, savanna market.
- Dark navy + yellow crescent moon = night.
- Deep indigo + simple roof shape = sleeping.
- **On-screen text:** Bold hand-lettered marker font, large, centered. Default RED. WHITE on a dark object. The place name and the year get their own text frame.
- **Signature devices:** A bold red X over the temple-and-treasure cliché; a split frame with the famous ruin small in the back and the worker large in front; a cloud thought bubble; a simple map dot, not a realistic map.
- **Color palette (approximate hex):** Orange #F58220 · Sky blue #7FB5D5 · Water blue #3D8EBF · Grass green #4E9A45 · Sand/tan #D2B488 · Indigo cloth #283A6E · Ochre #C47A3A · Red text #E0302B · Yellow #F4C430 · Cream #F5F1E6 · White #FFFFFF
- **Aspect ratio:** Always 16:9.

```
